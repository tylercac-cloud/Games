Token Screenerrug-risk checklist for new Solana tokens — not a buy signal, not a pump predictor
What this is, plainly This screens for the three documented rug-pull mechanisms and a handful of practitioner-sourced red flags. It cannot tell you whether a token will pump, gain attention, or be worth anything — no tool can, and anything claiming otherwise is not backed by the published research. What it can do: flag the structural setups that precede most on-chain fraud, which is a real and different question.

1 · Live on-chain check (optional)

Paste a mint address and this tries a direct, no-key call to Solana's public RPC, from your browser — not mine. It can only ever read three things this way: mint authority, freeze authority, and holder concentration. Everything else on this page has to be entered by hand, because it needs an indexer this RPC doesn't provide. This may be blocked by the sandbox you're running the page in; if so, the manual form below works exactly the same either way.

2 · Manual checklist

Look these up on DexScreener, Birdeye, RugCheck, or Solscan for the specific token. Leave anything you don't know as "Unknown" — the score only penalizes what's actually confirmed risky, never a blank.

Method & sources

Where every point value comes from, and what's a citation versus a heuristic.

Documented rug-pull mechanisms (large, fixed penalties)
A manually-verified benchmark of 117 confirmed Solana rug pulls classified every case into exactly three mechanisms: Pump-and-Dump (89 cases), Liquidity Withdrawal (26 cases), and Freeze Authority Abuse (2 cases). Source: "From Hype to Collapse: Investigating Rug Pull Scams on Solana," arXiv 2603.24625. Active mint authority, active freeze authority, and an unlocked liquidity pool map directly onto these three mechanisms - they aren't guesses, they're the literal preconditions for each documented category. A separate large-scale study (6.4M tokens) found the majority of Solana memecoins show rug-pull characteristics within the first hour, and that gradient-boosted models using only the first 5 minutes of trading data reach AUCPRC around 0.76-0.80 detecting them. Source: "Catching the Rug," arXiv 2608.20271.
Heuristic factors (smaller penalties, practitioner-sourced)
Holder concentration, liquidity depth, and deployer wallet share are cited across multiple practitioner red-flag guides (DeFade, BarryGuard) as commonly-checked signals, but I don't have a peer-reviewed, calibrated weight for any of them - the academic papers use trained ML models on many more features than a person can hand-collect, not a simple points table. These are included because they're genuinely part of the public conversation on this, labeled at the weight I judged reasonable, not derived from a study. Take them as somewhat less reliable than the three items above.
Token age - deliberately not scored
The two papers point in different directions here. One finds most rugs happen within the first hour (implying "new" is dangerous); another finds rug-pull risk in its dataset rises gradually the longer a token has been trading past its early phase (implying "new" is comparatively safer, for the subset that's still around to measure). I don't have a way to reconcile these into one honest number, so age is shown for context only and never subtracts or adds points.
What this explicitly cannot do
No published research - academic or industry - supports predicting which token gains attention or price. Multiple sources, including teams actively building AI trading products for this space, state directly that pump prediction doesn't work reliably; that's the field's own conclusion, not caution added here. A high score here means "not obviously structured to rug" - it says nothing about whether anyone will ever buy this token.
